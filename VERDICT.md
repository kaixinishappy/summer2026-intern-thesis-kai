# FinTech Disruption of Traditional Banking

**Thesis question:** Digital payment platforms, neobanks, and embedded finance players have taken significant market share from legacy institutions. Thesis evaluate whether FinTech disruption has run its course or whether the second wave of AI-native financial services is just beginning

## Verdict

The **first wave of fintech** has largely run its course. Between 2010 and 2021, payment platforms, neobanks, and challengers like PayPal, Block, and Robinhood won on user experience, not a fundamentally different banking model. Many turned unprofitable after 2021 while rising rates strengthened bank earnings, and traditional institutions have since reclaimed the pricing power and market position fintech briefly took. This is backed by price and profitability data across multiple years, and by three converging robustness checks: fintech's share of the combined revenue pool stayed essentially flat (~19% → 20%, 2022-2025) even as the challengers returned to profit — a direct market-share read, not just a stock-price proxy; the fintech basket underperformed even the broad Nasdaq-100 growth benchmark (QQQ), so the decline is fintech-specific rather than the 2022 growth-stock selloff; and a fintech-sector-ETF-vs-bank-sector-ETF check shows the same rise-and-rollover shape beyond the 7 tickers picked here.

The **second wave, AI-native finance**, can't be evaluated with the same confidence. Most AI-first firms are private or too newly public for meaningful data, so there's no way yet to tell lasting advantage from early hype. Search interest and AI-related filings show only modest upticks starting 2024. That thinness is itself the finding: too early to call, not absent.

Taken together, **incumbent banks look better positioned to absorb AI than to be displaced by it — though the sharpest new evidence adds a caveat.** Counting filings, banks disclose AI as actively as any challenger, so the "banks are too slow" narrative has little support. But *reading* the 2026 filings rather than counting them tells a more specific story: the three big banks (JPMorgan, HSBC, Barclays) name agentic AI almost entirely as a **risk or competitive threat** — disintermediation, cyber exposure, liability — not as something they are deploying, while the only genuine **deployment** language in the sample comes from the older embedded-finance players, PayPal and Block. So on the one direct read of who is *building* versus *fearing* agentic AI, it is the fintechs building and the banks flagging risk. This is one LLM's classification of 19 passages, not a hard statistic, but it cuts against the "banks absorb the wave" prediction more than for it.

The project's main empirical contribution is separating these two waves rather than treating fintech disruption as one continuous story. Wave 1 and Wave 2 run on different dynamics and different timelines, and blending them risks misattributing what's actually happening.


## Bottom line

**Fintech didn't die, and AI hasn't clearly taken over anything yet.** By the measures available here (price, profitability, search attention), the first wave has been substantially reabsorbed by the banks it once threatened, most visible in the 2022 net-income split where challengers posted losses the same year bank profits grew, and confirmed at the broader sector level (FINX vs. KBWB) so it isn't just the 7 tickers picked. The second wave is too early to call with market data, since the relevant companies aren't public; what thin evidence exists (EDGAR `agentic` language, search interest) is consistent with a real, early inflection starting around 2024, not nothing happening. The smart bet, though not something this project can prove, is that banks capture the AI wave the same way they captured the first one: they have the profits to fund it, and their names are on the filings that mention it. The one caveat worth flagging is that when you read those filings instead of counting them, the banks are naming agentic AI as a *threat*, and it's the older fintechs (PayPal, Block) whose filings actually describe deploying it — so if banks do win the AI wave, this early evidence isn't yet showing them leading it.
